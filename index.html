About

About the series

Agentic artificial intelligence is rapidly moving from proof-of-concept demonstrations toward practical tools for scientific research. In the life sciences, emerging systems can already support tasks ranging from literature synthesis and experimental planning to molecular design, data analysis and laboratory automation.

The AiLIFE: Accelerating Life Sciences with AI and Agents seminar series brings together life scientists, AI researchers and practitioners interested in how these technologies can accelerate scientific discovery. The series covers a broad range of topics, including AI agents, AI-guided experimentation, literature-scale reasoning, foundation models, generative design, automated laboratories and practical agentic workflows.

AiLIFE is intended both as a seminar series and as a forum for an emerging community of researchers who are already applying AI in their work, as well as those exploring how it could transform their research. Through talks by local and international experts and practitioners, we aim to share concrete advances, discuss open challenges and exchange ideas about how agentic AI can be used effectively in the life sciences.

We look forward to building a community around this rapidly evolving field and to exploring, together, how AI can help accelerate the scientific process.

At a glance

Who it is for
The broad life-science community, from AI-curious biologists and clinicians to ML researchers. No AI background required.
Format
One hour, monthly. Each session opens with a 10–15 minute AiLIFE Radar, a rotating round-up by one of the organizers of what has happened in AI in the past month that life scientists should know about, followed by the invited talk and discussion.
Where
Biomedicum Helsinki 1 (Meilahti campus), with sessions also planned on the Viikki campus. Every seminar is streamed on Zoom.

How to attend

Hosts

Organized and supported by

AiLIFE is organized by the MLBioMed (Machine Learning in Biomedicine) group at the Institute for Molecular Medicine Finland (FIMM), together with colleagues from the HiLIFE Biodata Analytics unit, the Precision Systems Medicine group and the Institute of Biotechnology on the Viikki campus. The series is part of the Helsinki Institute of Life Science (HiLIFE) and is funded by HiLIFE and FIMM, University of Helsinki.
